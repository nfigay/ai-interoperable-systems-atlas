# About the Author

![Nicolas Figay — author portrait](./images/author-linkedin-portrait.jpg)

## Research and expertise

The Atlas is developed from the perspective of an **interoperability expert, researcher, and author**, with doctoral (PhD) and habilitation (HDR) research focused on **continuous operational interoperability**. This perspective emphasizes the ability of independently evolving systems and organizations to sustain meaningful cooperation over time—not merely to establish an initial technical connection.

The author's work addresses interoperability as a multidisciplinary engineering and research problem, including enabling technologies, architectural approaches, standards, semantic alignment, and the challenges introduced by disruptive technologies.

## Research questions behind the Atlas

A central concern is understanding how new technologies can extend existing interoperability capabilities without obscuring the contributions and constraints of established approaches. AI makes that concern particularly urgent: it may support integration and knowledge work while also introducing new challenges for correctness, explainability, reproducibility, trust, and governance.

The Atlas therefore approaches AI as **both a possible enabler and a possible disruptor**. The goal is to examine how AI capabilities relate to legacy and established technologies that they do not automatically replace, and to assess those relationships through explicit models, evidence, and experiments.

The Atlas complements these publications with linked explanations, evidence-aware architectural views, learning journeys and progressively executable demonstrations. In particular, the work examines whether AI technologies **enable**, **disrupt**, or **change the requirements for** interoperability, and how their claims compare with established approaches they do not automatically replace.

## Purpose of this work

This Atlas is intended to help researchers, engineers, architects, and decision-makers ask more precise questions: what is being claimed, which kind of interoperability is involved, what is actually implemented, what evidence exists, and which assumptions remain untested?
